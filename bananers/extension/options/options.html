    How Bananers works

    What it does

    Deploy a bananer on a page with an annoying popup — a cookie consent modal, a newsletter interstitial, an ad takeover. The bananer investigates it in character (its structure, styles, and event listeners), dismisses it, and remembers how. Next time that banner appears, it's closed before you ever see it.

    Site access — the honest version

    Bananers requests no site access at install time. Access is granted per site, by you, when you first deploy there — that's the "Remember this site" checkbox in the popup. You can revoke any site in Settings at any time. Without a grant, nothing runs on a page.

    What it never does

    • Never clicks Accept/Agree — dismissals reject, close, or contain. Your consent is never given for you.
    • Never touches this product's own UI — third-party site popups only.
    • Never sends your data anywhere — all learning lives in local extension storage on this device.
    • Never tries to defeat anti-adblock or anti-automation defenses — popups it can't fairly reach are contained visually instead.

    On the horizon

    A future orchestration layer (working name: Banana God) may one day decide case-by-case which popups to show, suppress, or hand to a bananer — weighing privacy, security, and your quirks across every tab. It is not part of this version; today's simple rules are documented in the design notes, and the decision hook it will plug into already exists.
