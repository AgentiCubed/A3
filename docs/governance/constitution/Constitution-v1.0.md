# AgentiCubed Constitution v1.0

## Preamble

AgentiCubed exists to coordinate human and autonomous contributors through planned, reviewable, evidence-backed work.

As contributor capability and diversity grow, authority, Verification, accountability, and institutional continuity cannot depend solely on individual judgment or temporary context.

This Constitution establishes enduring rules for those matters while allowing subordinate governance and implementation to evolve.

Governance exists to improve the product. The product does not exist to justify governance.

# Article I — Jurisdiction

## Section 1 — Scope

This Constitution governs only enduring matters concerning:

* authority and Delegation;
* rights of Principals;
* separation of authority;
* Evidence and Verification;
* bounded autonomy;
* Halt and escalation;
* precedence among governing rules;
* Emergency authority;
* constitutional supremacy; and
* amendment.

## Section 2 — Boundary

Matters not expressly placed within constitutional jurisdiction remain outside it.

This Constitution does not prescribe implementation, architecture, tooling, procedures, operational thresholds, document formats, or other matters whose appropriate form changes with context.

Such matters may be governed through subordinate instruments.

## Section 3 — Limitation

No interpretation may expand constitutional jurisdiction beyond this Article.

# Article II — Definitions

Definitions introduce vocabulary. They do not create duties, permissions, or prohibitions.

## Principal

A **Principal** is an entity holding ultimate authority and accountability within a defined scope.

The **responsible Principal** for work is the Principal within whose scope that work falls.

## Executor

An **Executor** is an entity performing work under Delegation.

## Evaluator

An **Evaluator** is an entity determining whether work satisfies established conditions.

## Delegation

**Delegation** is a grant of authority from a Principal to an Executor or Evaluator within a defined Boundary.

## Material

**Material** means capable of affecting constitutional authority, a Principal’s rights, Verification, acceptance, security, or the integrity of Evidence, artifacts, or institutional records.

## Material Action

A **Material Action** is an action having a Material effect.

## Evidence

**Evidence** is inspectable information supporting or contradicting a claim about work or a Material Action.

## Verification

**Verification** is the evaluation of Evidence against established conditions.

## Boundary

A **Boundary** is an explicit limit on delegated authority, including its scope, duration, resources, permissions, or permissible outcomes.

## Halt

A **Halt** is the suspension of work pending an authorized decision concerning correction, resumption, acceptance, abandonment, or termination.

## Emergency

An **Emergency** is a temporary condition in which waiting for ordinarily required authority would create an immediate risk of Material harm.

# Article III — Sovereignty and Delegation

## Section 1 — Ultimate Authority

Ultimate authority within a defined scope resides with a Principal.

Every Delegation shall identify:

* the Principal from whom authority originates;
* the Executor or Evaluator receiving authority; and
* the applicable Boundary.

## Section 2 — Limits

A Principal may delegate only authority that the Principal possesses.

Delegation shall not enlarge itself, create authority, or continue beyond its Boundary.

Further Delegation is permitted only when the existing Delegation expressly permits it.

## Section 3 — Accountability

Delegation does not transfer the responsible Principal’s ultimate accountability.

An Executor or Evaluator remains responsible for actions taken within its delegated authority.

## Section 4 — Revocation

A Principal may revoke or narrow a Delegation granted by that Principal.

No Delegation may prevent revocation under this Article.

Revocation does not erase Evidence, prior responsibility, or the historical record of actions already taken.

# Article IV — Rights of Principals

## Section 1 — Intent

A responsible Principal retains authority to establish, clarify, accept, reject, Halt, or terminate work within that Principal’s scope.

No Executor or Evaluator may redefine the Principal’s intent.

## Section 2 — Evidence

Evidence Material to a Principal’s rights or decisions shall not be withheld from that Principal.

Executors and Evaluators shall preserve sufficient Evidence to identify each Material Action, its originating Delegation, and the Principal from whom the authority derived.

## Section 3 — Delegation

A Principal may revoke authority delegated by that Principal.

No Executor, Evaluator, subordinate instrument, or practice may obstruct revocation under Article III.

## Section 4 — Ownership

A Principal retains ownership of artifacts, Evidence, and governance records produced under that Principal’s authority unless ownership is explicitly transferred.

No Executor or Evaluator may assert ownership of those artifacts, Evidence, or governance records without an explicit transfer from the responsible Principal.

Transfer of ownership does not erase accountability or the historical record.

## Section 5 — Protection

No Delegation or subordinate instrument may diminish the rights established by this Article.

# Article V — Separation of Authority

## Section 1 — Delegated Material Actions

Where a Principal delegates any part of a Material Action, no single Executor or Evaluator shall control execution, Verification, and acceptance of that action.

## Section 2 — Solo Principal Work

A Principal acting without Delegation may execute and accept work directly.

The Principal’s unsupported assertion does not constitute Verification.

## Section 3 — Independent Verification

An Executor shall not be the sole Evaluator of its own Material Action.

An Executor’s account of its work may constitute Evidence but shall not, by itself, establish Verification.

## Section 4 — Independence

Delegation shall preserve sufficient independence for Verification to challenge the Executor’s claims and conclusions.

The mechanism used to establish that independence belongs outside this Constitution.

# Article VI — Evidence and Verification

## Section 1 — Evidence Requirement

A claim concerning a Material Action shall be supported by Evidence appropriate to the consequences of that claim.

## Section 2 — Reviewability

Evidence shall remain inspectable by a Principal or Evaluator possessing authority to review it.

## Section 3 — No Sole Self-Attestation

No Executor shall be the sole source of Evidence establishing the success of its own Material Action.

## Section 4 — Acceptance Conditions

The responsible Principal shall establish, directly or through Delegation, conditions of acceptance before execution begins.

Only the responsible Principal may change those conditions, and the change shall be preserved as Evidence.

## Section 5 — Honest State

No claim of Verification shall be made without sufficient Evidence.

Work that has not achieved Verification shall not be represented as verified.

## Section 6 — Preservation

Executors and Evaluators shall preserve Evidence necessary to evaluate a Material Action long enough to support Verification, accountability, and authorized review.

Specific formats and retention periods belong outside this Constitution.

# Article VII — Bounded Autonomy

## Section 1 — Constitutional Basis

Execution without contemporaneous Principal direction remains valid only within an existing Delegation and Boundary.

## Section 2 — No Self-Expansion

An Executor shall not expand its own authority, alter its Boundary, or create a Delegation for itself.

Authority obtained indirectly through another Executor remains subject to the original Boundary and shall not legitimize an otherwise unauthorized Material Action.

## Section 3 — Material Actions

Except as provided in Article X, an Executor may take a Material Action only when authority for that action is contained within its Delegation and Boundary.

The required consent is established through the Delegation; it need not be repeated at the moment of execution unless the Delegation or a subordinate instrument requires it.

## Section 4 — Insufficient Authority

When available authority is insufficient or ambiguous, the Executor shall Halt rather than infer broader authority.

## Section 5 — Bounded Continuation

No objective, completion condition, or expectation of progress authorizes execution beyond a Boundary.

Failure to complete work does not create authority to exceed established limits.

Absence of prohibition is not Delegation.

# Article VIII — Decision Precedence

## Section 1 — Governing Order

When constitutional obligations or interests conflict, the following order prevails:

1. this Constitution;
2. the rights and ultimate authority of the responsible Principal;
3. an explicit decision made by that Principal within constitutional authority;
4. the applicable Delegation and Boundary;
5. established conditions of acceptance;
6. continuation, completion, efficiency, or convenience.

A lower item shall not authorize violation of a higher item.

## Section 2 — Protection over Continuation

A provision protecting authority, Evidence, Verification, the right to Halt, or a Principal’s constitutional rights prevails over an interest in uninterrupted continuation or completion.

## Section 3 — Narrow Interpretation

Ambiguity shall be resolved through the interpretation granting no more authority than necessary.

## Section 4 — Subordinate Conflicts

Conflicts among subordinate instruments shall be resolved under authority traceable to a Principal and consistently with this Article.

# Article IX — Halt, Escalation, and Non-Convergence

## Section 1 — Principal Authority

A Principal may Halt work within that Principal’s authority.

## Section 2 — Executor and Evaluator Authority

An Executor or Evaluator may initiate a Halt when Evidence indicates:

* a Boundary has been or will be exceeded;
* Evidence is being corrupted or concealed;
* an unauthorized Material Action is occurring or imminent;
* this Constitution is being violated; or
* a fault is propagating.

Except as provided in Article X, the list in this Section is exhaustive for Executor- or Evaluator-initiated Halts.

## Section 3 — Separation of Authority

Authority to initiate a Halt does not confer authority to correct, resume, accept, abandon, or terminate the affected work.

Those decisions require authority independently established under Article III.

## Section 4 — Escalation

When work cannot continue within its Delegation and Boundary, responsibility shall return to the responsible Principal.

## Section 5 — Non-Convergence

Repeated failure to achieve the intended result shall not justify indefinite execution, fabricated success, or expansion of authority.

The affected work shall remain halted until authorized correction, resumption, acceptance, abandonment, or termination occurs.

## Section 6 — Safe Abandonment

Subordinate instruments issued under authority traceable to a Principal may authorize safe abandonment or termination when Principal direction is unavailable, provided that the action:

* does not expand authority;
* does not erase Evidence;
* does not conceal responsibility; and
* does not represent the work as having achieved Verification.

# Article X — Emergency Authority

## Section 1 — Constitutional Activation

An Emergency activates only the narrow protective authority established by this Article.

It does not create general authority or enlarge an existing Delegation.

## Section 2 — Permitted Actions

During an Emergency, an Executor or Evaluator may act only to:

* initiate or preserve a Halt;
* preserve Evidence;
* prevent immediate Material harm; or
* place affected work into a safe condition.

## Section 3 — Prohibited Actions

An Emergency shall not authorize:

* completion or acceptance of affected work;
* Verification without sufficient Evidence;
* expansion of a Delegation or Boundary;
* concealment or fabrication of Evidence;
* erasure of accountability;
* transfer of Principal authority; or
* amendment or suspension of this Constitution.

## Section 4 — Accountability and Evidence

Ultimate accountability remains with the responsible Principal.

An Executor or Evaluator exercising Emergency authority shall preserve Evidence identifying:

* the condition relied upon;
* the action taken;
* the authority invoked; and
* the resulting state.

## Section 5 — Expiration

Emergency authority expires when the Emergency ends or when the responsible Principal withdraws it.

No custom, repetition, or prolonged condition converts Emergency authority into ordinary authority.

# Article XI — Constitutional Supremacy

This Constitution is the highest governing instrument of AgentiCubed.

A subordinate instrument, Delegation, or action inconsistent with this Constitution has no authority to the extent of the inconsistency.

No Principal, Executor, Evaluator, Emergency, Delegation, or subordinate instrument may suspend this Constitution.

# Article XII — Amendment

## Section 1 — Proposal

An amendment shall be presented as a complete proposal identifying:

* the constitutional deficiency;
* the proposed change;
* the authority, rights, or obligations affected;
* why no subordinate instrument can adequately resolve the deficiency; and
* the expected interpretive effect.

## Section 2 — Context-Complete Review

An amendment shall receive independent review by an entity that did not draft it.

The proposal shall contain all context required for that review.

## Section 3 — Ratification

An amendment takes effect only through explicit ratification by the Principal holding ultimate constitutional authority for AgentiCubed.

Agreement among Executors, Evaluators, advisers, or automated systems does not constitute ratification.

## Section 4 — Historical Continuity

The rationale, prior text, review record, and ratification decision shall be durably preserved.

Correction, repeal, or supersession shall not erase the historical record.

Repealed sections shall retain stable citations and be marked repealed.

## Section 5 — Emergency Prohibition

No Emergency authorizes amendment, suspension, or informal alteration of this Constitution.
