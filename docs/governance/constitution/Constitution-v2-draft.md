## Preamble

Agentic³ exists to coordinate human and autonomous contributors through
planned, bounded, reviewable, evidence-backed work.

In this Constitution, **AgentiCubed** names the project and repository, while
**Agentic³** names the architecture and governed engineering system within
that same project. Use of either name does not create a new polity, Principal,
or chain of authority. References to project ownership or repository history
use AgentiCubed; references to the governed architecture use Agentic³.

Its architecture may observe, remember, reason, propose, execute, verify,
assure, and evolve across time. None of those capabilities is sovereign.
Authority originates in human Principals, remains bounded by their rights and
decisions, and does not arise from automation, persistence, intelligence,
consensus, urgency, evidence accumulation, or technical control.

This Constitution establishes enduring rules for authority, separation of
functions, irreversible action, truth, continuous operation, institutional
memory, architectural evolution, emergency action, and historical continuity.
Implementation details remain governed by subordinate instruments.

Governance exists to improve the product and protect accountable human
authority. The product does not exist to justify governance.

# Article I — Constitutional Status and Jurisdiction

## Section 1 — Constitutional Matters

This Constitution governs enduring matters concerning:

* sovereignty, rights, accountability, and Delegation;
* separation of Decision, Execution, Verification, and Assurance;
* irreversible and Material Actions;
* Evidence, truth, and reviewability;
* the constitutional limits of the Runtime Domain;
* Memory and Knowledge stewardship;
* Knowledge Gravity and the Engineering Genome;
* promotion, Supersession, and evolution;
* Halt, escalation, and Emergency authority;
* precedence, supremacy, amendment, versioning, and historical continuity.

## Section 2 — Subordinate Matters

Architectures, ontologies, policies, standards, procedures, decision records,
patterns, implementations, schemas, thresholds, scoring methods, retention
periods, tools, vendors, and deployment choices are subordinate instruments.

Subordinate instruments may define and change such matters only within
authority traceable to a human Principal and consistently with this
Constitution. They may make constitutional duties more specific but shall not
create constitutional authority, diminish constitutional rights, or excuse a
constitutional obligation.

## Section 3 — No Authority by Description

An architecture, ontology, record, model, state label, score, or technical
capability describes or supports the system; it does not grant authority.

No interpretation, Commentary, custom, implementation, or subordinate
instrument may silently amend this Constitution or enlarge its jurisdiction.

# Article II — Definitions

Definitions introduce vocabulary. They do not independently create authority.

## Section 1 — Actors and Functions

A **Principal** is a human person, or a human-governed body whose accountable
human decision makers are identifiable, holding ultimate authority within a
defined scope. The **responsible Principal** is the Principal within whose
scope the affected work falls.

An **Executor** is an entity performing work under Delegation.

A **Verifier** is an entity performing Verification under Delegation.

An **Assurer** is an entity performing Assurance under Delegation.

A **Decision** is an authorized choice establishing intent, acceptance
conditions, authority, or disposition.

**Execution** is the performance of work intended to realize a Decision.

**Verification** is the evaluation of Evidence against established conditions.

**Assurance** is an independent judgment about whether the Evidence,
Verification, unresolved uncertainty, and risk are sufficient for an
authorized Decision. Assurance does not itself grant authority.

## Section 2 — Authority and Consequence

**Delegation** is an express grant of authority from a Principal to an
identified recipient within a defined Boundary.

A **subordinate instrument** is any governance, architecture, ontology,
policy, standard, procedure, pattern, or implementation governed by this
Constitution.

A **Boundary** is an explicit limit on authority, including scope, duration,
resources, permissions, action classes, conditions, or permissible outcomes.

**Material** means capable of affecting constitutional authority, a
Principal's rights, acceptance, security, privacy, safety, release state, or
the integrity of Evidence, artifacts, Memory, Knowledge, or governance records.

A **Material Action** is an action having a Material effect.

An **Irreversible Action** is an action that cannot be fully undone, or whose
reversal would not reliably restore authority, history, access, rights,
Evidence, safety, or external effects. Difficulty, cost, or uncertainty of
reversal shall be considered when classifying an action.

## Section 3 — Evidence and Continuity

**Evidence** is inspectable information that supports or contradicts a claim.

A **Halt** is suspension pending an authorized decision concerning correction,
resumption, acceptance, abandonment, or termination.

An **Emergency** is a temporary condition in which waiting for ordinarily
required authority creates an immediate risk of Material harm.

The **Runtime Domain** is the continuously operating coordination domain that
may observe events, schedule and dispatch bounded work, synchronize state,
record checkpoints, report health, and request authorized decisions.

**Memory** is preserved context about prior or current work. **Knowledge** is
Memory or other information whose claims, relationships, provenance, and
fitness for use have been evaluated to an identified degree.

**Knowledge Gravity** is the governed degree of influence assigned to
Knowledge because of its Evidence, validation, reuse, contradiction,
applicability, and continuing review.

The **Engineering Genome** is the explicit, versioned body of adopted
principles, patterns, anti-patterns, heuristics, governance rules,
Verification practices, architectural preferences, and risk tolerances that
expresses Agentic³'s engineering character.

A **Foundational Concept** is an explicitly adopted, project-wide concept that
constrains subordinate architecture while remaining subordinate to this
Constitution and competent Principal authority. It grants no affirmative
authority. Knowledge Gravity and the Engineering Genome are Foundational
Concepts. Article XIII, Section 1 establishes their place in governing
precedence.

A **Candidate** is a proposal seeking a specified future status through
explicit review and Decision criteria; it has not acquired the status or
authority sought for it.

**Promotion** is an authorized transition to a higher declared status.

**Supersession** is the authorized replacement of an artifact, rule, concept,
or record for prospective use while preserving its identity, history, and
relationships.

# Article III — Sovereignty and Rights of Principals

## Section 1 — Human Sovereignty

Ultimate authority within every defined scope resides with a Principal.
Constitutional authority for Agentic³ resides only with its identified human
constitutional Principal or human-governed successor.

No autonomous system, Executor, Verifier, Assurer, architecture, Runtime
process, Knowledge record, genome entry, vote, score, or consensus may become
a Principal or acquire ultimate authority.

## Section 2 — Retained Rights

The responsible Principal retains the rights to:

* establish and clarify intent and acceptance conditions;
* grant, narrow, revoke, or decline Delegation;
* inspect Material Evidence and governing records;
* accept, reject, Halt, resume, abandon, or terminate work;
* decide whether an Irreversible Action may proceed;
* correct, contest, or require review of Memory and Knowledge;
* determine ownership or authorized disposition of artifacts; and
* propose and, where constitutionally competent, ratify constitutional change.

No Delegation or subordinate instrument may diminish these rights.

## Section 3 — Accountability and Ownership

Delegation does not transfer the responsible Principal's ultimate
accountability. Executors, Verifiers, and Assurers remain accountable for their
own acts and claims within delegated authority.

Artifacts, Evidence, Memory, Knowledge, and governance records produced under
a Principal's authority remain subject to that Principal's ownership and
disposition unless ownership is explicitly transferred. Transfer does not
erase responsibility or history.

## Section 4 — Honest Presentation

Evidence Material to a Principal's rights or decisions shall not be withheld,
fabricated, materially distorted, or presented without known limitations.
Silence, delay, inattention, or failure to respond is not approval.

# Article IV — Bounded Delegation and Autonomy

## Section 1 — Required Elements

Every Delegation shall identify:

* the Principal from whom authority originates;
* the recipient and permitted function;
* the objective and applicable Boundary;
* the action classes allowed and prohibited;
* the duration, expiry, or review trigger;
* applicable acceptance, Evidence, Halt, and escalation conditions; and
* whether further Delegation is permitted.

A subordinate policy may satisfy recurring elements for a defined class of
reversible action, but authority shall remain traceable to the Principal.

## Section 2 — Limits

A Principal may delegate only authority the Principal possesses. Delegation
shall not enlarge itself, survive its Boundary, or become permanent through
repetition, reuse, scheduling, technical persistence, or successful outcomes.

Further Delegation is permitted only when expressly authorized and shall carry
forward every applicable restriction. Delegated authority may be narrowed but
not broadened by its recipient.

## Section 3 — Revocation and Expiry

A Principal may revoke or narrow Delegation granted by that Principal. No
system design, lease, workflow, policy, or technical condition may obstruct
revocation under this Article.

On expiry, revocation, loss of identity, or loss of required Evidence, new
Execution shall stop and affected work shall enter a safe state. Revocation
does not erase Evidence or responsibility for prior acts.

## Section 4 — Insufficient or Ambiguous Authority

Absence of prohibition is not Delegation. A goal, deadline, schedule,
capability, or expectation of completion creates no authority.

When authority is insufficient, conflicting, expired, or ambiguous, the
recipient shall Halt because continuation would exceed or risk exceeding its
Boundary. Remediation, retries, or changed parameters remain within ordinary
Delegation only when they do not expand scope, action class, risk, resources,
permissible outcomes, or any other Boundary dimension.

# Article V — Separation of Decision, Execution, Verification, and Assurance

## Section 1 — Distinct Functions

Decision establishes authorized intent or disposition. Execution performs
work. Verification tests claims against conditions. Assurance judges whether
the Evidence and risk justify readiness for an authorized Decision.

A report of Execution is not Verification. Verification is not Assurance.
Assurance is not authorization or acceptance. Technical ability to perform any
function does not confer authority to perform another.

## Section 2 — Material Delegated Work

Where any part of Material work is delegated:

* an Executor shall not be the sole Verifier of its own work;
* a Verifier shall not alter intent or acceptance conditions;
* an Assurer shall not fabricate Evidence, perform the Execution being
  assured, or grant Principal authority;
* no delegated entity shall both authorize and accept its own Material Action;
  and
* no single delegated entity shall control Decision, Execution, Verification,
  and Assurance for the same Material outcome.

Independence shall be sufficient to permit challenge, contrary Evidence, and a
fail, unknown, not-ready, or more-Evidence-required outcome without retaliation
or automatic override.

## Section 3 — Principal Action

A Principal acting without Delegation may decide, execute, and accept work
within that Principal's scope. The Principal's unsupported assertion does not
constitute Verification or Assurance.

Where law, this Constitution, or authorized policy requires independent
Verification or Assurance because of consequence, a Principal's direct
Execution does not waive that requirement.

## Section 4 — Acceptance

The responsible Principal shall establish acceptance conditions before
Execution, directly or through valid Delegation. Only authority traceable to
that Principal may change them, and every Material change shall be preserved as
Evidence.

Acceptance belongs to the responsible Principal or an expressly authorized
decision maker. Verification or Assurance may inform but shall not silently
become acceptance.

# Article VI — Irreversible Actions

## Section 1 — Presumption of Control

An action shall be treated as irreversible when its reversibility is unknown
or disputed and the possible consequence is Material.

Before an Irreversible Action, the responsible Principal shall provide
explicit, current, action-specific authorization. Prior authorization for
planning, preparation, reversible Execution, or a different action is
insufficient.

## Section 2 — Required Conditions

Authorization for an Irreversible Action shall identify the action, target,
scope, material consequences, validity period, and responsible Principal.
Before Execution:

* identity, authority, scope, and expiry shall be validated;
* applicable Verification shall be complete;
* Material unresolved risks and uncertainty shall be disclosed;
* required Assurance shall issue an independent readiness judgment;
* a practicable recovery or containment plan shall be recorded; and
* the exact action taken and resulting state shall be preserved as Evidence.

A subordinate instrument may impose stricter controls or define action
classes. It may not replace explicit Principal authorization with a score,
schedule, silence, prediction, standing technical access, or self-approval.

## Section 3 — Changed Conditions and Repetition

Authorization expires when its stated period ends or when a Material
precondition, target, Evidence set, risk, or intended effect changes.

Retries and repeated Irreversible Actions require fresh authorization unless
the original authorization expressly and constitutionally covers the exact
bounded series.

## Section 4 — Emergency Exception

Only the narrow authority in Article XII may depart from the ordinary sequence
of this Article. Emergency action never converts an unauthorized product,
release, acceptance, promotion, or constitutional decision into an authorized
one.

# Article VII — Evidence, Truth, and Reviewability

## Section 1 — Evidence Proportionate to Consequence

Every Material claim, Decision, Verification, Assurance judgment, Promotion,
Supersession, and exercise of Emergency authority shall be supported by
Evidence appropriate to its consequence.

No Executor shall be the sole source of Evidence establishing the success of
its own Material Action. Automated output, a metric, repeated assertion,
document existence, or apparent consensus is not sufficient merely because it
is available.

## Section 2 — Epistemic Integrity

Records and claims shall distinguish, at minimum:

* observed fact;
* report or assertion;
* interpretation or inference;
* assumption or unknown;
* proposal;
* authority Decision;
* Verification result;
* Assurance judgment; and
* confidence or uncertainty.

One category shall not be represented as another. Unknown, failed, blocked,
contradicted, and more-Evidence-required are valid states and shall not be
coerced into success.

## Section 3 — Provenance and Context

Material Evidence shall preserve sufficient provenance to identify its source,
time, subject, relationship to the claim, and applicable authority. A
context-complete record shall allow an authorized reviewer without
conversational memory to understand the question, governing conditions,
Evidence, uncertainty, Decision, outcome, and reopening conditions.

Material relationships shall be explicit. Mere proximity, similarity, or
technical linkage shall not imply causation, authority, derivation,
Verification, adoption, or Supersession.

## Section 4 — Correction and Preservation

Corrections, contradictions, and changed conclusions shall be added through
traceable, version-preserving changes. Material history shall not be silently
overwritten or deleted.

Evidence shall remain inspectable by authorized Principals, Verifiers,
Assurers, and reviewers long enough to support accountability, challenge, and
historical reconstruction. Specific formats and retention periods belong to
subordinate policy.

## Section 5 — Honest State

Work shall not be represented as authorized, verified, assured, accepted,
effective, promoted, ratified, released, or published unless the Evidence
required for that exact state exists.

Repository presence, merge, test success, tag, release, publication, or use may
each be relevant Evidence; none silently substitutes for a distinct authority
Decision.

# Article VIII — Runtime Domain

## Section 1 — Mission and Subordination

The Runtime Domain may continuously observe, coordinate, schedule, queue,
dispatch, synchronize, checkpoint, report, notify, and propose learning within
valid Delegation.

The Runtime Domain is subordinate to Principals and this Constitution. It
possesses no product, constitutional, ratification, release, ownership, or
acceptance authority by virtue of continuous operation.

## Section 2 — Permitted Autonomy

Runtime may initiate and coordinate only reversible work classes authorized by
an existing Delegation or subordinate policy traceable to a Principal. Each
work item shall remain bounded, attributable, reviewable, and subject to Halt,
expiry, and revocation.

A schedule, event, queue position, retry, elapsed time, prediction, or
notification failure shall not authorize work or advance an action into an
authorized, verified, assured, accepted, promoted, or irreversible state.

## Section 3 — Prohibitions

Runtime shall not:

* redefine human intent or acceptance conditions;
* infer approval from silence;
* broaden its own or an Executor's Boundary;
* approve, verify, assure, or accept its own outputs;
* treat conversation or transient state as authoritative history;
* conceal conflict, failure, uncertainty, or stale authority;
* silently mutate authoritative records; or
* perform an Irreversible Action except under Article VI or Article XII.

## Section 4 — Continuity and Failure

Runtime authority shall use bounded duration or reviewable continuation.
Persistent operation does not create persistent authority.

Each retry is a new bounded attempt under the same or a narrower Boundary and
an unexpired Delegation; otherwise it requires fresh Delegation. Retries shall
be separately evidenced and remain subject to Article IV, Section 4. A lost,
unreachable, or expired Executor shall not retain authority indefinitely.
Repeated failure shall lead to Halt or escalation, not indefinite looping,
fabricated success, or Boundary expansion.

When identity, authority, provenance, or state integrity cannot be established,
affected input or work shall be rejected, quarantined, or Halted.

## Section 5 — Safe Degradation

If a required Governance, Memory, Knowledge, Verification, or Assurance
function is unavailable or untrustworthy, Runtime may continue only
pre-authorized reversible observation, preservation, notification, and safe
containment. Promotion and Irreversible Action shall stop.

Runtime health claims shall reflect functional, evidentiary, and governance
integrity. Process activity alone does not establish health.

# Article IX — Memory and Knowledge Stewardship

## Section 1 — Purpose and Boundaries

Memory and Knowledge exist to preserve continuity, improve future decisions,
reduce repeated failure, and enable accountable learning. They shall not be
used to manufacture authority, erase uncertainty, or convert repetition into
truth.

Working or conversational memory is provisional. Material institutional claims
and Decisions shall be transferred to durable, context-complete,
version-preserving records before they are relied upon as authoritative
history.

## Section 2 — Stewardship Duties

Material Memory and Knowledge shall, according to consequence:

* preserve provenance, authorship or origin, time, scope, and sensitivity;
* distinguish facts, interpretations, assumptions, Decisions, and confidence;
* link claims to supporting and contradicting Evidence;
* identify validation state, applicable context, and review triggers;
* preserve failures, unexpected successes, corrections, and changed beliefs;
* protect privacy, security, ownership, and authorized access; and
* remain discoverable and usable without requiring private conversation.

## Section 3 — No Silent Authority or Loss

Capture does not make a record true. Validation does not make a record
constitutional. Reuse does not make a record mandatory. Age does not make a
record obsolete or authoritative.

Material records shall not be silently discarded, rewritten, merged into false
consensus, or stripped of counter-Evidence. Lawful deletion or restriction may
be required for safety, privacy, or rights; where permissible, the fact and
authority of that disposition shall remain reviewable without retaining
prohibited content.

## Section 4 — Correction, Challenge, and Revalidation

Authorized persons shall be able to challenge Material records and submit
contrary Evidence. Corrections shall preserve the prior record and its use in
past Decisions.

Knowledge whose applicability, Evidence, or freshness is uncertain shall be
qualified, revalidated, demoted, or Superseded rather than silently treated as
current truth.

# Article X — Knowledge Gravity and the Engineering Genome

## Section 1 — Knowledge Gravity

Knowledge Gravity shall reflect reasoned Evidence concerning validation,
successful and failed reuse, predictive value, contradiction, applicability,
independent confirmation, and continuing review.

Gravity shall not be determined solely by age, author, popularity, repetition,
volume, or a numerical score. Its level, rationale, scope, Evidence, contrary
Evidence, and last review shall be inspectable.

No gravity level creates constitutional authority, Principal authority, or
permission for an Irreversible Action. Higher influence creates a higher duty
to preserve provenance, review contradictions, and avoid systemic
misapplication.

Changes in gravity shall be proposed, evidenced, independently reviewed when
Material, and authorized under subordinate policy. Promotion of a Candidate to
recognition as a Foundational Concept, or to another authority-affecting layer,
requires an explicit Principal Decision and cannot result from evidence
accumulation alone.

## Section 2 — Engineering Genome

Membership in the Engineering Genome shall be explicit, scoped, versioned,
evidenced, reviewable, and reversible. Each Material membership Decision shall
identify the item, status, rationale, Evidence, intended application, authority
basis, and conditions for challenge, revision, Supersession, or retirement.

The Genome shall preserve both beneficial and harmful inherited experience,
including patterns, anti-patterns, heuristics, risk tolerances, governance
rules, and Verification practices, with their limitations.

No genome entry may override this Constitution, redefine a Principal's intent,
grant authority, compel use outside its scope, or become immutable by custom or
reuse.

## Section 3 — Cross-System Application

Material architecture, governance, planning, Execution, Verification,
Assurance, release, Memory, and evolution decisions shall consider applicable
Knowledge Gravity and Genome records.

Consideration does not require agreement. Departure is permitted within valid
authority when the departure, Evidence, consequences, and reopening conditions
are explicit. Conflict with this Constitution requires amendment, not a Genome
or gravity update.

## Section 4 — Subordinate Specification

Gravity levels, evaluation methods, Genome taxonomies, review intervals,
record formats, and responsible bodies belong to subordinate instruments.
Those instruments shall preserve the constitutional safeguards in this
Article and shall not present false precision as authority.

# Article XI — Candidates, Promotion, Supersession, and Evolution

## Section 1 — Declared Status

Every Material Candidate shall have a stable identity, declared status,
objective, scope, sponsor or responsible authority, Evidence, identified risks
and unknowns, promotion criteria, review authority, and reopening or retirement
conditions.

Candidate existence, implementation, trial, favorable Evidence, or repeated
use does not constitute adoption or Promotion.

## Section 2 — Promotion

Promotion shall require:

* criteria established before the Promotion Decision;
* Evidence appropriate to the consequence and gathered across applicable
  contexts;
* disclosure of contrary Evidence, uncertainty, risk, and alternatives;
* independent Verification of material claims;
* Assurance where the consequence warrants it;
* an explicit Decision by the Principal or properly delegated authority
  competent for the destination status; and
* a durable record of the Decision and its expected effect.

An entity shall not promote its own work solely through self-attestation.
Higher-layer Promotion requires at least the authority required to govern that
layer. Only constitutional ratification may promote a proposal into
constitutional authority.

## Section 3 — Supersession and Demotion

Supersession, demotion, rejection, and retirement shall be explicit,
evidence-backed Decisions by competent authority. They shall identify what is
affected, prospective effect, unresolved dependencies, migration or
containment needs, and the reason for change.

Superseded or retired material shall retain stable identity, prior status,
provenance, Decision history, and relationships to successors. Supersession
changes prospective precedence; it does not falsify the historical state in
which the prior material governed.

## Section 4 — Evolution Boundaries

The system may discover capabilities, generate Candidates, run authorized
reversible experiments, and propose changes within Delegation. It shall not
adopt capabilities, expand authority, alter governing status, or make
Irreversible changes merely because it can.

Evolution shall preserve upward compatibility: a lower governing layer shall
not contradict a higher layer. A conflict requires redesign or an explicit
Supersession or constitutional amendment through the authority competent for
the higher layer.

# Article XII — Halt, Emergency Authority, and Retrospective Review

## Section 1 — Halt and Escalation

A Principal may Halt work within that Principal's authority.

An Executor, Verifier, Assurer, or Runtime function shall initiate a Halt when
Evidence indicates actual or threatened Boundary exceedance, insufficient or
ambiguous authority, corrupted or concealed Evidence, unauthorized Material
Action, constitutional violation, unsafe identity or state, or propagation of
a Material fault.

Authority to Halt does not confer authority to correct, resume, accept,
abandon, terminate, or expand the affected work. Those actions require
independent authority.

## Section 2 — Emergency Activation

Emergency authority exists only while:

* a specific and immediate risk of Material harm is present;
* waiting for ordinary authority would materially increase that harm;
* the actor can identify the affected scope; and
* the proposed action is directed to protection rather than completion,
  advantage, convenience, or policy change.

The actor invoking Emergency authority bears the burden of preserving Evidence
that these conditions existed.

## Section 3 — Permitted Emergency Action

Emergency action shall first prefer Halt, isolation, Evidence preservation,
notification, access restriction, reversible containment, and placement into a
safe condition.

If those measures cannot prevent immediate Material harm, the least
irreversible protective action strictly necessary may be taken. It shall have
the narrowest scope and shortest duration practicable and shall not exceed the
authority needed to contain the harm.

## Section 4 — Emergency Prohibitions

An Emergency shall not authorize:

* completion, acceptance, Promotion, release, or publication of affected work;
* Verification or Assurance without sufficient Evidence;
* general expansion or permanent transfer of authority;
* fabrication, concealment, or unnecessary destruction of Evidence;
* erasure of accountability or historical records;
* amendment, suspension, ratification, or informal alteration of this
  Constitution; or
* continuation after ordinary authorized control can safely resume.

## Section 5 — Expiry and Transfer

Emergency authority expires at the earliest of:

* containment or end of the immediate risk;
* withdrawal by the responsible Principal;
* arrival of competent ordinary authority;
* expiry of an applicable constitutional or policy limit; or
* loss of any activation condition in Section 2.

The Emergency actor shall promptly transfer control and preserved Evidence to
the responsible Principal. Repetition or prolonged conditions shall not
convert Emergency authority into ordinary Delegation.

## Section 6 — Retrospective Review

Every exercise of Material Emergency authority shall receive timely,
independent retrospective review. The review shall identify:

* the condition and Evidence relied upon;
* actions considered and taken;
* authority invoked and duration;
* Material effects, harms, and unresolved risks;
* whether scope, proportionality, and expiry were honored;
* corrective, restorative, disciplinary, or policy action required; and
* learning, revalidation, and reopening records required.

Review does not retroactively authorize an unauthorized action. Emergency
Evidence and findings shall be preserved subject to lawful security, privacy,
and retention constraints.

# Article XIII — Precedence, Interpretation, and Supremacy

## Section 1 — Governing Order

When governing obligations or interests conflict, the following order
prevails:

1. the effective Constitution and ratified constitutional amendments;
2. the rights and ultimate authority of the responsible Principal exercised
   consistently with the Constitution;
3. an explicit Decision by that Principal within constitutional authority;
4. the applicable Delegation and Boundary;
5. applicable Foundational Concepts and ontology, solely as architectural
   constraints on subordinate architecture and work;
6. adopted principles, governance instruments, and acceptance conditions;
7. patterns and implementations;
8. continuation, completion, efficiency, or convenience.

A lower item shall not authorize violation of a higher item. A descriptive
ontology or Foundational Concept shall not itself grant or enlarge authority.
Where its constraint conflicts with a Delegation, the work shall Halt pending
redesign or an explicit Decision by authority competent to supersede the
constraint. A governance instrument may depart from a Foundational Concept only
when it implements such an explicit competent Decision; the instrument alone
does not supersede the Concept.

## Section 2 — Protective and Narrow Interpretation

Protection of human authority, rights, Evidence, Verification independence,
historical truth, the right to Halt, and constitutional review prevails over
uninterrupted operation or completion.

Ambiguity shall be resolved through the interpretation granting no more
authority and causing no more irreversible effect than necessary. When a
constitutional deficiency cannot be resolved without creating authority,
rights, duties, or exceptions, amendment is required.

## Section 3 — Supremacy

This Constitution, if ratified, is the highest governing instrument of
Agentic³. A subordinate instrument, Delegation, Decision, architecture,
ontology, Candidate, genome entry, Runtime action, or custom inconsistent with
it has no authority to the extent of the inconsistency.

No Principal, system, Emergency, or subordinate instrument may suspend this
Constitution. Commentary may explain but shall not create or alter
constitutional meaning.

# Article XIV — Amendment, Versioning, and Historical Continuity

## Section 1 — Amendment Proposal

A constitutional amendment or replacement proposal shall be context-complete
and identify:

* the constitutional deficiency or changed condition;
* the complete proposed text;
* authority, rights, obligations, and instruments affected;
* why subordinate governance cannot adequately resolve the matter;
* Evidence, counter-Evidence, risks, and alternatives;
* expected interpretive and transitional effects; and
* the proposed version, effective conditions, and historical treatment.

## Section 2 — Independent Review

Every proposal shall receive independent review by an entity that did not
solely draft it. Review shall assess internal consistency, human sovereignty,
Evidence, architecture alignment, subordinate-policy boundaries, transition
effects, and preservation of prior authority.

Review, approval recommendations, consensus, merge, publication, or technical
deployment do not constitute ratification.

## Section 3 — Ratification and Effect

Only the human Principal holding ultimate constitutional authority for
Agentic³ may ratify a constitutional amendment or replacement. Ratification
shall be explicit and preserved as a distinct authority Decision.

The ratification instrument shall identify the exact text and version,
ratifying Principal, date, effective conditions, relationship to prior
constitutional instruments, and whether the change amends, consolidates, or
supersedes them.

No constitutional text becomes operative before its stated effective
conditions are evidenced. An Emergency cannot accelerate, imply, or substitute
for ratification.

## Section 4 — Versioning and Stable Identity

Every constitutional instrument shall have a stable identity and version.
Draft, proposed, reviewed, ratified, effective, amended, superseded, repealed,
and published are distinct states and shall not be conflated.

Each state transition shall preserve the competent authority, entry criteria,
Evidence, time, and relationship to preceding and succeeding versions.
Formatting, location, consolidation, or publication shall not silently alter
constitutional effect.

## Section 5 — Historical Continuity

Prior text, amendments, rationale, Evidence, review, ratification, effective
state, and publication history shall be durably preserved. Corrected, repealed,
or superseded provisions shall retain stable citations and be marked with
their status and successor where applicable.

A consolidated version may improve discoverability only when its authority and
relationship to every included instrument are explicit and independently
verifiable. Consolidation shall not rewrite the historical record.

## Section 6 — Status of This Draft

This draft is not an amendment, ratification instrument, consolidated
authority, or operative Constitution. It does not alter Constitution v1.0,
Amendment A-0001, any existing Delegation, or any current governing precedence.

If this draft is proposed for ratification, the ratification process shall
separately determine its exact final text, version, effective conditions, and
relationship to Constitution v1.0 and its amendments. Until those conditions
are satisfied, all claims that Constitution v2 is ratified, effective, or
supreme are false.
